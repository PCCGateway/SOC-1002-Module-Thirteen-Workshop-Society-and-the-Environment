TASK 1

Launch: Environment as a Social Issue

Big Question: How do human behavior, social institutions, power, and inequality shape environmental problems?

Environmental sociology studies the relationship between society and the natural and built environment. Environmental problems are not only problems of nature; they are connected to decisions made by individuals, corporations, governments, and communities.

People

Choices about transportation, consumption, energy, water, and waste.

Institutions

Government, economy, education, media, law, and corporations shape what choices are possible.

Power & Inequality

Environmental benefits and environmental burdens are not always distributed equally.

TASK 3

Apply the Three Sociological Paradigms

The same environmental issue can look different depending on the theoretical lens we use. Apply all three to the issue you selected.

Functionalism

Focus: stability, institutions, functions, interdependence, intended and unintended consequences.

Ask: What role does this practice or institution play? How are parts of society connected? What happens when the system does not function well?

From a functionalist perspective, this issue shows how ___ and ___ are interconnected because...

Conflict Theory

Focus: power, inequality, competition for resources, wealth, decision-making, who benefits and who bears costs.

Ask: Who has power? Who profits? Who experiences the risk? Whose voice is heard?

Conflict theory draws attention to inequality between ___ and ___ because...

Symbolic Interactionism

Focus: meanings, labels, symbols, identity, everyday interaction, and how people define a situation.

Ask: What meanings do people attach to the issue? How do language, media, identity, or social interactions shape behavior?

A symbolic interactionist might examine how people define or talk about ___ and how that meaning influences...
